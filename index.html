05Methods

How the Atlas is built

Data

In this prototype every respondent is simulated: a seeded generator draws each person's country, tradition, demographics, schemas, horizon, and answer text from distributions chosen to resemble plausible survey data. Nothing here is a real response.

In the live Atlas the corpus is the Aim 1 survey: about 1,000 adults in each of fifteen countries, answering the same open-ended prompts in their own language, with the Future Imagination and Traditions Inventory and present-outcome measures. New waves and new countries are added as the research program grows.

Scoring

Each response is scored on six dimensions, each 0 to 100: temporal horizon, richness, content and valence, agency, social boundary, and narrative form. In the live Atlas, classifiers trained on a human-coded subset produce these scores and are validated by language and by country before any number is shown. Schemas are the images, obligations, and story forms a response draws on, coded against the project codebook.

Future similarity index

A visitor's answers are compared with every respondent as a weighted sum: 0.3 overlap of schemas and themes (text and ticked boxes), 0.12 each for closeness of horizon, who is included, hopefulness, and agency, 0.12 for matching narrative form, and 0.10 for richness. People above 0.72 are counted as sharing the visitor's future. In the live Atlas the text comparison uses the same multilingual sentence embeddings as the research, so a description in any language can be placed beside futures written in any other.

Privacy

Respondents are shown only as de-identified avatars with age band, gender, income band, education, affiliation, and a place at the level of a city or region. Answers are shown as written but with names and identifying details removed before deposit. Visitors' answers to the questionnaire are compared in the browser and never stored.
